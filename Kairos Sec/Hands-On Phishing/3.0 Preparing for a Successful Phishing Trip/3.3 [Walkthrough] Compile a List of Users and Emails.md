## Compile a List of Users and Emails

### Overview

In this lesson, we build on foundational OSINT skills by exploring how to extract pretextual and technical intelligence directly from a target’s public-facing website. Students analyze the simulated domain `hacksmarter-manufacturing.shop` to identify user data, organizational context, and phishing opportunities—all without active engagement.

This type of reconnaissance is essential for crafting effective phishing scenarios in social engineering engagements.

### Key Concepts

- **Simplicity in OSINT** OSINT doesn’t have to be complex. Simply reviewing the content on a target's website can reveal user names, job roles, services, technologies in use, and company history—all of which can inform phishing strategy.
- **Extracting Pretext from Business Language**  
    The target website provides insights that can inform phishing pretexts:
    - 24/7 system monitoring → Alert-themed phishing emails
    - Consulting and project management services → Pretending to be a vendor or PM
    - Testimonials and newsletter references → Building trust or following up on fake events
- **Using Company History**  
    The “Our Story” section reveals:
    - Office locations (e.g., Toledo, Ohio; Austin, Texas)
    - Company timeline and milestones
    - Industry affiliations and client focus  
        These can all serve as pretext elements to enhance believability.
- **Email and User Enumeration**  
    The “Team” page includes user names, roles, and a visible email format pattern (`[firstinitiallastname@hacksmarter.org](mailto:firstinitiallastname@hacksmarter.org)`). This allows attackers to enumerate target addresses quickly and accurately.
- **Mobile App Consideration**  
    Even references to mobile apps (real or not) can be used to justify cloned login pages or alerts in phishing campaigns. The assumption that apps may be less secure is a common entry point for attackers.
- **Passive Target Analysis**  
    No active scanning or fuzzing is necessary. Students are reminded to keep their enumeration passive to avoid unintentional service disruption.

### Practical Application

- **Login Page Discovery Challenge**  
    The lesson concludes with a challenge: use all collected information and the knowledge that the site runs on **WordPress** to locate a login page that could be cloned for a phishing campaign. This reinforces the connection between OSINT discovery and technical exploitation.
- **Real-World Relevance**  
    The walkthrough models how professional red teamers approach reconnaissance: collecting subtle but meaningful details from marketing content, technical pages, and organizational structure to craft well-informed phishing attacks.

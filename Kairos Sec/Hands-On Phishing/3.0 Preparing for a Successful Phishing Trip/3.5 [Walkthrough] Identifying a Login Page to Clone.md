## Identifying a Login Page to Clone

### Overview

In this lesson, students learn the importance of identifying a legitimate login page that can be cloned as part of a phishing engagement. The exercise emphasizes the role of common content management systems (CMS) like WordPress and the value of targeting login interfaces used regularly by employees.

This task forms the foundation for creating a convincing phishing landing page later in the engagement.

### Key Concepts

- **Why Clone a Login Page?**  
    Cloning a real login page improves the realism of a phishing campaign. When employees are directed to a spoofed page that mimics a familiar interface, they are more likely to enter their credentials. This can result in successful credential harvesting and subsequent access to internal systems.
- **Types of Login Portals to Target**  
    Common login portals include:
    - Admin and CMS logins (e.g., WordPress)
    - Email or webmail portals
    - Internal tools or dashboards
    - Client, support, or vendor portals
- **Using CMS Knowledge for Discovery**  
    In this scenario, the target website (`hacksmarter-manufacturing.shop`) is confirmed to use WordPress. Knowing this, students can test common WordPress login paths:
    - `/wp-login.php`
    - `/wp-admin/`
- The lesson demonstrates successfully locating the login form by visiting `/wp-login.php`, confirming a viable phishing target.
- **Spoofing Considerations**
    - Simplicity: WordPress login pages are clean, minimal, and easy to replicate.
    - Flexibility: They accept both email addresses and usernames, increasing the chance of capturing valid credentials.
    - Realism: Familiar branding and structure help reduce suspicion.

### Practical Takeaways

- Always begin by identifying whether the target site uses a CMS, then test default login paths associated with that CMS.
- When cloning a login page, choose one that is regularly used by the target’s employees and allows credential submission.
- Note and document any discovered login paths during reconnaissance. In this case:  
    **Login Path:** `/wp-login.php`

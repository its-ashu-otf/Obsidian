## Overview

Before launching your own phishing campaigns, it’s crucial to understand what ineffective and poorly crafted phishing emails look like. In this lab, you will analyze examples of common phishing mistakes and begin building a mental checklist to ensure your own phishing emails are realistic, convincing, and professionally crafted.

This exercise will help you distinguish between obvious red flags and subtle indicators that trigger suspicion among recipients.

### Review the Common Mistakes in Phishing Emails

Study the following key characteristics of weak or poorly crafted phishing emails:

**Generic Greetings**

- Examples: "Dear Customer", "Dear IT Employee"
- These fail to personalize the message and raise immediate suspicion.

**Grammatical and Typographical Errors**

- Frequent errors damage credibility and make the email appear unprofessional.

**Low-Resolution or Inaccurate Branding**

- Avoid using distorted or pixelated logos. Use high-quality images that match the target brand.

**Suspicious Sender Addresses**

- Never send from public domains like `@gmail.com` for corporate phishing scenarios. Set up a domain that closely mimics the target organization.

**Unusual or Mismatched URLs**

- Long or random URLs that don’t resemble the legitimate domain quickly get flagged.

**Overt Requests for Sensitive Info**

- Asking directly for usernames, passwords, or personal information by email is an immediate red flag.

**Language and Tone Issues**

- Phrases like "Kindly send..." are often found in scam emails. Avoid unnatural or overly formal language that sounds inauthentic.

## Strategies for Selecting a Domain

### Overview

Selecting the right domain is one of the most critical components of a successful phishing engagement. This lesson explores strategies for purchasing domains that closely resemble the target organization’s domain in a way that appears legitimate to the human eye, increasing the likelihood of victim interaction.

Students are introduced to concepts like typosquatting, character substitution, subdomain spoofing, and top-level domain (TLD) manipulation—all of which can be used to create deceptive but believable phishing URLs.

---

### Key Concepts

- **Domain Similarity Matters** Phishing success often hinges on visual believability. A domain that appears nearly identical to the legitimate one helps avoid detection and encourages user interaction with phishing emails and spoofed login pages.
- **Typosquatting (Domain Permutations)**  
	![[Pasted image 20251212214455.png]]
    This involves intentionally registering domains with minor spelling errors or typographic changes (e.g., "hacksmurtter-manufacturing.shop"). Users may overlook these differences due to habitual reading patterns and mental autocorrection.
- **Look-Alike Characters**  
	![[Pasted image 20251212214540.png]]
    Using similar-looking characters (e.g., replacing 'i' with '1', or 's' with '5') can bypass casual inspection, especially in long or complex domain names. These are often effective in email addresses and URLs.
- **Subdomain Spoofing via Hyphenation**  
	![[Pasted image 20251212214629.png]]
    Rather than purchasing a domain like `login.example.com`, attackers can register domains such as `login-example.com` to simulate subdomains. The dash placement creates a believable impersonation.
- **Alternative Top-Level Domains (TLDs)**  
	![[Pasted image 20251212214706.png]]
    When the original site uses a `.com`, an attacker may register `.cam`, `.co`, `.biz`, etc. For example, `hacksmarter-manufacturing.cam` may pass casual inspection while being fully under the attacker's control.

---

### Pro Tips and Observations

- **.cam for .com Spoofing**  
    The `.cam` TLD looks nearly identical to `.com` and is often unregistered by target organizations. It can be purchased cheaply and used effectively in phishing engagements.
- **Readability Advantage**  
    Small changes are often missed by users skimming emails or URLs. This makes low-cost spoof domains a high-return tactic in real-world red team scenarios.
- **Registration Recommendations**  
    Although domain registration is optional in this course, it is highly encouraged. Doing so allows students to follow through on advanced tasks involving SSL certificates, phishing page deployment, and email configuration.

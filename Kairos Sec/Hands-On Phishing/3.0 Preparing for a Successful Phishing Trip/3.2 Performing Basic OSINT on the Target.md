### Overview

This lesson introduces the concept and practical application of **Open Source Intelligence (OSINT)** as it relates to phishing and social engineering engagements. OSINT refers to gathering publicly available information to profile a target organization without direct interaction or intrusion. The focus in this segment is on identifying user data, email formats, subdomains, and the underlying technologies used by the target website.

### Key Concepts

- **Definition of OSINT**: Open Source Intelligence involves collecting data from publicly accessible sources such as websites, LinkedIn profiles, WHOIS data, and internet-wide tools to support offensive security operations.
- **Passive vs. Active Enumeration**:
    - _Passive OSINT_ avoids interacting with the target system directly (e.g., search engines, third-party tools).
    - _Active Enumeration_ includes direct probing or scanning (e.g., `nmap`) and is discouraged during this stage.
- **OSINT Targets for Phishing**:
    - Employee/user identification
    - Email address formats and providers
    - Email security filters and infrastructure (e.g., Gmail, Microsoft 365)
    - Public-facing subdomains
    - Login portals or CMS-based admin areas

### Tools Demonstrated

- **WhatCMS.org**  
    Used to detect the content management system of a site. In this case, the target domain `hacksmarter-manufacturing.shop` is identified as running **WordPress 6.8.1**.
- **BuiltWith**  
    Another passive tool for identifying technology stacks and configurations used by a target domain.
- **OWASP AMASS**  
    A powerful tool for passive subdomain enumeration and asset discovery. Demonstrated both against the lab site and a live target (`tryhackme.com`) with a bug bounty program, AMASS revealed multiple subdomains and potential vectors for phishing engagement.

### Practical Guidelines

- Do not perform active scans or fuzzing on the target domain. All enumeration in this stage should be non-intrusive and passive.
- Focus your research on building a realistic profile of the target organization using only publicly available data.
- When identifying CMS platforms like WordPress, consider that known login pages could be cloned for use in phishing payloads.

### Key Takeaway

Effective phishing campaigns begin with strategic information gathering. Understanding the structure, users, and technologies of a target allows red teamers to craft convincing and high-success-rate phishing payloads.

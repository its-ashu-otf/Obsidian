# Lab Guide: Setting Up Firewall Rules for Your Ubuntu Droplet

In this lab, you’ll apply **basic but critical security hardening** to your DigitalOcean droplet. You’ll configure a firewall to allow SSH access **only from your own IP address**, reducing the risk of unauthorized access — even if your private key is somehow exposed.

---

## Why Set Up a Firewall?

- Even with **SSH key-based login**, anyone can attempt to brute-force your SSH port if it's publicly exposed.
- Cloud VMs are frequently scanned by bots — minimizing your attack surface is essential.
- This is true for both **Blue Teams** (defense) and **Red Teams** (protecting infrastructure from other attackers).

> **Best Practice:** Combine SSH key authentication with strict IP-based firewall rules.

---

## Step-by-Step: Create a Firewall in DigitalOcean

### 1. Navigate to Your Droplet’s Settings

1. From the **Droplet dashboard**, click on your droplet.
2. Click the **"Networking"** tab.
3. Scroll down to the **"Firewalls"** section.
4. Click **“Edit”** (or **“Create Firewall”** if none exists).

---

### 2. Modify the SSH Rule

By default, SSH (port 22) is open to **all IPs** (`0.0.0.0/0`), which is dangerous.

#### Get Your IP Address:

In your **Kali terminal** or any shell:

`curl ifconfig.me`

Copy the IP it returns — this is your public IP address.

> 🧠 **Important:** If you're using a VPN or your IP changes (e.g., mobile hotspot), you'll need to update this later.

#### Update the Rule:

- Under **Inbound Rules**, find the SSH rule.
- Replace `0.0.0.0/0` with **your public IP address**.
- Click **Add Source**, paste your IP, and hit Enter.

---

### 3. Tag Your Firewall

- Name the firewall something like: `ssh-my-ip-only`
- Under **"Apply to Droplets"**, use the tag from earlier:  
    `phishing`
- This ensures the firewall auto-applies to any droplet with that tag.

Click **Create Firewall**.

---

## Verification: Test SSH Access

### While on Your Whitelisted IP:

Run from Kali or any terminal:

`ssh root@<your_droplet_ip>`

You should connect successfully.

---

### Switch IPs (e.g., Disable VPN):

1. Turn off your VPN or switch networks.
2. Try the same SSH command again:

ssh root@<your_droplet_ip>

❌ It should **fail silently** — SSH will appear closed.

> Even port scans (e.g. `nmap`) will show port 22 as **filtered or closed**.

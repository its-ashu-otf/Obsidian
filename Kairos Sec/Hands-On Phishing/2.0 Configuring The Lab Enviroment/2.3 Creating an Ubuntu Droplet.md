# ☁️ Lab Guide: Creating an Ubuntu Droplet in DigitalOcean

Your cloud-based phishing infrastructure begins with a **virtual server**. In DigitalOcean, these virtual machines are called **Droplets**. In this lab, you'll spin up your first **Ubuntu Droplet**, configure SSH authentication, and verify remote access.

---

## Step 1: Create a Project

1. In the DigitalOcean control panel, go to **Projects** → click **"New Project"**.
2. Name:  
    `Hands-On Phishing Course`
3. Description:  
    `This project is for the Hands-On Phishing course through Kairos Sec.`
4. Purpose:  
    Select **Class Project / Educational**
5. Click **Create Project**
6. On the “Move resources” screen → click **“Skip for now”**

---

## Step 2: Create the Droplet

1. Inside your new project, click **“Create”** → **“Droplet”**
2. Follow the steps in the video for the Droplet configuration.

---

## Step 3: Add SSH Key Authentication

### Why SSH Keys?

SSH keys are more secure than passwords — they protect against brute-force and credential stuffing attacks.

### Generate a Key (from your Kali VM):

```
ssh-keygen
```

- Hit **Enter** to accept the default location.
- Leave **passphrase** blank (press Enter twice).

To extract your **public key**, run:

```
cat ~/.ssh/id_ed25519.pub
```

Copy the output.

### Add it to DigitalOcean:

1. In the SSH section of droplet setup, choose **SSH Key**.
2. Click **"New SSH Key"**.
3. Paste your public key.
4. Name it something like:  
    `kali-ssh-key`
5. Click **"Add SSH Key"**

---

## Step 4: Final Setup Options

- **Hostname**: Keep the default or name it `phishing-lab`.
- **Tags**: Add a tag:  
    `phishing`
- Click **Create Droplet**

Wait ~1 minute. A green status dot will indicate it’s live.

---

## Step 5: Connect to Your Droplet

1. Copy the **public IP address** from the droplet dashboard.
2. In your Kali terminal, connect via SSH:

`ssh root@<your_droplet_ip>`

> Example:  
> `ssh [root@134.122.45.89](mailto:root@134.122.45.89)`

On first connection, type `yes` to trust the host key.

# Security Auditing Process

### Security Auditing Lifecycle  

![[Pasted image 20250518214046.png]]

### 🔍 **1. Planning and Preparation**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Clearly define _why_ you're auditing—regulatory compliance (e.g., PCI DSS, ISO 27001), risk identification, internal policy adherence, etc.
        
    - Determine _scope_ tightly—avoid scope creep, which leads to wasted effort and legal/operational issues.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Always include out-of-scope systems in documentation for awareness, but label them clearly.


---

### 📋 **2. Information Gathering**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Study policies to see if what’s on paper matches what's being practiced.
        
    - Interview a diverse range of staff (not just IT/security) to uncover “tribal knowledge” and shadow IT.
        
    - Pull configs from firewalls, routers, Active Directory GPOs, etc.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Use automation where possible (e.g., scripts to pull system info or a config snapshot from endpoints).


---

### ⚖️ **3. Risk Assessment**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Use threat modelling (like STRIDE or DREAD) to assess how real-world adversaries would target critical assets.
        
    - Map vulnerabilities to MITRE ATT&CK techniques for better context.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Use CVSS scores as a baseline but adjust for business impact—technical severity ≠ business criticality.
    

---

### 🛠️ **4. Audit Execution**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Use a combination of tools (e.g., Nessus for vuln scans, Burp Suite for web apps, manual testing for misconfigs).
        
    - Test controls like password policies, MFA enforcement, logging, and incident response procedures.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Ensure chain of custody and evidence integrity if findings might lead to legal consequences or HR action.
    

---

### 📊 **5. Analysis and Evaluation**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Correlate findings across different sources (e.g., a misconfigured firewall rule and a known exposed system).
        
    - Benchmark against NIST, CIS Controls, or ISO 27001 depending on industry/regulations.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Use heatmaps or risk matrices to visually communicate impact to non-technical stakeholders.
    

---

### 📝 **6. Reporting**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Keep reports layered: Executive Summary (non-tech), Technical Report (detailed), and Action Plan.
        
    - Include **proof-of-concept** or **evidence** like screenshots or logs—this makes reports hard to ignore.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Keep language neutral and constructive. Don’t just say “X is broken”—say _how it affects the business_ and _how to fix it_.
    

---

### 🔧 **7. Remediation**

- **What to Do:**
    
    - Help prioritize fixes using a risk vs. effort matrix.
        
    - Guide implementation of security hardening, patching, network segmentation, etc.
        
    - Schedule a re-audit or validation testing session.
        
- **Pro Tip:**  
    Document lessons learned to improve future audits and reduce recurring findings.
    

---

### 🧠 Final Thoughts

The key to a solid security audit isn't just finding issues—it’s **driving meaningful change**. Good auditors provide **insight** and **enablement**, not just lists of problems.
